# Commercial Licensing

## Overview

Juris AI is open-source under the **AGPL-3.0-only**. This gives you
broad freedom to use, study, modify, and self-host the software.

AGPL-3.0's network-use clause (Section 13) means: if you modify Juris
AI and let other users interact with your modified version over a
network — including offering it as a hosted service — you must make
the corresponding source of your modified version available to those
users.

**A Commercial License removes this obligation**, for organizations
that need to keep their modifications, integrations, or a hosted
offering proprietary.

## When You Might Need a Commercial License

You likely need a commercial license if:

- You want to offer a **closed-source, modified version of Juris AI**
  as a hosted SaaS product to your own customers.
- You are delivering a **proprietary on-premise deployment** with
  modifications you cannot disclose.
- You are integrating Juris AI into a larger proprietary product and
  want a clear, negotiated basis for doing so rather than relying on
  AGPL's copyleft terms.
- You want **dedicated support** or contractual terms not offered
  under the open-source license.

## What a Commercial License Is Not

To be direct about where this project actually stands today:

- There is no existing commercial-license agreement template published
  here yet — terms are negotiated directly, case by case.
- This page does not constitute a license, an offer, or legal advice.
  Any actual commercial terms are set out in a separate, signed
  agreement negotiated with you.
- If you have specific legal questions about your situation under
  AGPL-3.0, consult your own counsel — this page and the README FAQ
  explain the general shape of the license, not your specific
  obligations.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I self-host Juris AI for my own firm's internal use?**
A: Yes, under AGPL-3.0, with no source-disclosure obligation — running
an unmodified copy internally, even as a network-accessible internal
tool for your own staff, doesn't trigger Section 13. If you *modify*
it and your staff interact with the modified version over a network,
Section 13 can require making that modified source available to
them — it stays within your organization; you're not required to
publish it publicly. See the README's licensing FAQ for more detail.
For your specific situation, consult your own counsel.

**Q: I want to offer Juris AI (or something based on it) as a hosted
product to my own customers. Do I need a commercial license?**
A: If you modify Juris AI and offer the modified version to your
customers over a network, AGPL-3.0 Section 13 would generally require
you to make the corresponding source available to those customers. If
you don't want that obligation, a commercial license is the
alternative — reach out using the contact above to discuss terms.

**Q: Does a commercial license exist yet, or is this exploratory?**
A: Commercial licensing is available and terms are negotiated
directly — there is no fixed price list or self-serve agreement yet.
Reach out via the email above to start a conversation.
